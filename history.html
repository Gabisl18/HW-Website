The History of Surfing

From ancient Polynesian ritual to a global sport — a brief timeline.

Timeline

c. 1000 CE

Wave-riding develops among Polynesian cultures, most notably in Hawaii, where surfing (he'e nalu) is deeply woven into social and spiritual life.

18th Century

Captain James Cook's expedition documents Hawaiian surfers, giving the Western world its first written accounts of the sport.

Early 1900s

Duke Kahanamoku, Olympic swimmer and native Hawaiian, travels internationally giving surfing demonstrations, helping popularize the sport in the U.S. and Australia.

1950s–60s

Lighter foam-and-fiberglass boards replace heavy wooden ones, sparking a surf culture boom in California and around the world.

1970s

The shortboard revolution introduces smaller, more maneuverable boards, changing the style and performance of modern surfing.

Today

Surfing is a global sport with professional tours, made its Olympic debut in 2021, and continues to evolve with new board designs and wave-riding disciplines.
